# Operating-margin audit

**Verdict:** The updated Base Case ties: 2027 revenue $5,866m less six operating-expense categories $3,313m equals $2,553m, or 43.5%. The 2028 result is 43.9%. These are analyst outputs, not guidance. [Quarterly engine](../data/forecast/forecast_quarterly.csv); [Q2 2026 release](https://investors.robinhood.com/news-releases/news-release-details/robinhood-reports-second-quarter-2026-results).

| Year | Revenue | Operating expense | Operating income | Margin | Expense growth |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2025 | $4,473m | $2,379m | $2,094m | 46.8% | — |
| 2026 | $5,191m | $2,969m | $2,222m | 42.8% | 24.8% |
| 2027 | $5,866m | $3,313m | $2,553m | 43.5% | 11.6% |
| 2028 | $6,325m | $3,546m | $2,779m | 43.9% | 7.0% |

2026 combines two **ACTUAL** and two **FORECAST** quarters. Category totals below include the same basis. The expense/revenue ratio is 57.2% in 2026, 56.5% in 2027 and 56.1% in 2028.

**Mechanics:** Brokerage cost has a fixed base plus a weighted transaction-unit basket. Operations partly follows funded customers. Credit provision follows modeled card balances. Marketing partly follows net customer additions. Technology and G&A follow visible quarterly budget steps rather than revenue percentages. These fixed-like and semi-variable classifications are analyst assumptions; Robinhood does not disclose product-level cost elasticities. [Forecast method](FORECAST_METHOD.md).

**Leverage:** 2027 revenue grows 13.0% and expense grows 11.6%; incremental GAAP operating margin is 49.0%. 2028 incremental margin is 49.2%. The reported 2024–2025 incremental margin was **68.3%**; the forecast does not carry it forward. Credit provision is a material rising expense in this model, while the 2026 comparison embeds H2 spend assumptions calibrated with the adjusted-expense outlook in view. [Phase 3 history](HISTORICAL_ANALYSIS.md); [FY 2025 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1783879/000178387926000029/hood-20251231.htm).

**Challenge:** A high forecast margin depends on transaction monetization, interest balance/yield paths and expense restraint occurring together. The model's 2026 adjusted expense comparison is a consistency check, not independent validation of 2027–2028 category budgets.
